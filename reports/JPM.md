## 2. Company Overview

JPMorgan Chase is the largest U.S. bank by assets and a global financial powerhouse operating across three segments: **Consumer & Community Banking** (retail deposits, cards, mortgages, auto, small business), **Commercial & Investment Bank** (markets/trading, M&A advisory, capital markets, treasury services, commercial lending), and **Asset & Wealth Management**. This diversification produces durable, counter-cyclical earnings: when markets are volatile, trading revenue rises; when rates fall, fee and mortgage activity tends to pick up.

JPM's competitive moat is built on **scale, funding cost advantage, and technology investment**. A vast, sticky low-cost deposit base funds its balance sheet cheaply, while ~$17B+ annual technology spend creates a widening gap versus regional and even peer money-center banks. The "fortress balance sheet" — high CET1 capital, strong reserves, and liquidity — allows JPM to take share organically and through downturns (e.g., the First Republic acquisition), and to consistently return capital via dividends and buybacks. Its ~16.5% ROE materially exceeds its ~10% cost of equity, the hallmark of genuine value creation.

**Key risks:** sensitivity to the credit cycle (rising charge-offs in a recession), net-interest-margin compression if the Fed cuts aggressively, regulatory/capital requirements (Basel III "endgame"), litigation and operational/cyber risk, and the law of large numbers limiting growth at >$4T in assets.

## 4. Valuation — Discount Rate (Cost of Equity)

For a bank, the appropriate discount rate is the **cost of equity**, not WACC (debt/deposits are operating inputs). Derived via CAPM:

| Input | Value | Source / Rationale |
|-------|-------|--------------------|
| Risk-Free Rate (Rf) | 4.50% | 10-yr US Treasury |
| Equity Risk Premium (ERP) | 5.50% | Damodaran consensus |
| Beta (β) | 1.023 | Reported (stock data) |
| **Cost of Equity (Ke = Rf + β×ERP)** | **10.13%** | CAPM — used as discount rate |
| Effective Tax Rate | 21.0% | Fallback (not disclosed); ~corporate norm |

*Note: A naive WACC is not used. Setting D/V = 0 in the WACC engine recovers the pure CAPM cost of equity (10.13%), which is the correct discount rate for equity-based bank valuation.*

### Method 1 — Residual Income (Excess Return) Model
Value = Book Value + PV of future economic profits, where economic profit = (ROE − Ke) × Book.

- Year-1 residual income = (0.1647 − 0.1013) × $128.38 = **$8.14/share**
- PV (growing perpetuity at g=5%) = 8.14 / (0.1013 − 0.05) = **$158.7**
- **Intrinsic value = 128.38 + 158.7 = ~$287**

### Method 2 — Justified Price-to-Book
Justified P/B = (ROE − g) / (Ke − g) = (0.1647 − 0.05) / (0.1013 − 0.05) = **2.24x**
- **Intrinsic value = 2.24 × $128.38 = ~$287**

### Method 3 — Justified / Multiple Price-to-Earnings
Using the economically-consistent total payout (retention = g/ROE = 30%, i.e. ~70% total payout via dividends + buybacks):
- Leading justified P/E = 0.70 × 1.05 / (0.1013 − 0.05) = **~14.3x**
- On forward FY EPS ($22.31): **~$319**; on TTM EPS ($20.89): **~$298**
- Simple peer-relative range (13–14x × +1y EPS $23.55): **$306 – $330**

## 9. Investment Risks

**Bull Case (toward ~$340–391, +14% to +31%):**
- ROE sustains at 16–17% and the market awards a higher through-cycle P/B (capital return + share gains compound book value per share).
- Soft-landing macro: resilient NII, benign credit costs, and a capital-markets/investment-banking rebound drive earnings beats.
- Basel "endgame" finalized more leniently than feared, unlocking excess capital for accelerated buybacks.

**Bear Case (toward ~$240–265, −12% to −20%):**
- Recession lifts credit charge-offs and reserve builds; ROE compresses toward 12–13%, collapsing the justified P/B premium (see sensitivity: Ke 11% / g 4% → ~$229).
- Aggressive Fed cuts compress net interest margin faster than fee income can offset.
- Heightened regulation/capital requirements, litigation, or a cyber/operational event impairs returns.

## 10. Verdict

**Recommendation: HOLD** (with a constructive long-term bias), **target range $290–$325, base case ~$305.**

JPMorgan is, by almost any measure, the highest-quality large-cap bank in the world — a fortress balance sheet, diversified earnings engine, elite ~16.5% ROE, and disciplined capital return. The problem is not the business; it is the price. Three independent, conservative frameworks (Residual Income and Justified P/B at ~$287; earnings-based methods at ~$300–330) converge on an intrinsic value of roughly **$305**, only modestly above the current $299. At 2.3x book and ~14x trailing earnings, the market is already paying full price for the franchise's superiority, leaving a thin margin of safety.

I would **not chase the stock at current levels** but would happily **own it through the cycle** and **add aggressively into weakness** — the sensitivity grid shows compelling value emerges below ~$265 (a Ke ~11% / g ~4% scenario). The bull-case path to the $342 consensus requires JPM to *sustain* peak-level ROE and growth, which is plausible but not the base case given normalizing rates and the law of large numbers.

**Catalysts to watch:** quarterly NII trajectory and 2026 guidance, credit/charge-off trends, Basel III endgame finalization, buyback pace, and capital-markets activity. **Time horizon:** 12–24 months. **Net:** A core, lower-risk holding trading near fair value — HOLD, accumulate on dips toward the high-$260s/low-$280s.
