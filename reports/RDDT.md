## 2. Company Overview

Reddit, Inc. operates one of the world's largest community-driven social platforms, organizing user-generated conversation into hundreds of thousands of topic-based "subreddits." The company monetizes primarily through advertising (the overwhelming majority of revenue), supplemented by a fast-emerging, high-margin **data-licensing** business in which Reddit sells access to its vast corpus of human conversation to train AI/LLM models. This second revenue stream — effectively selling a proprietary, hard-to-replicate dataset — is a structurally attractive, high-incremental-margin business that differentiates Reddit from peers.

Reddit's competitive moat rests on its network effects and the irreplaceable nature of its content: two decades of authentic, human-written discussion across virtually every conceivable interest. This makes its data uniquely valuable in the AI era and gives its advertising inventory strong contextual targeting potential. The company is still early in monetization — average revenue per user remains a fraction of mature peers like Meta and Pinterest — leaving a long runway for ARPU expansion, international growth, and ad-load optimization.

Key risks include: extreme dependence on Google Search referral traffic (algorithm changes can swing user metrics violently), an unproven long-term advertising track record relative to incumbents, governance/community tension (moderators and users can revolt against monetization), the durability of one-time data-licensing deals, and a very high stock beta reflecting elevated volatility and sentiment-driven trading.

The principal caveat on earnings quality is that profitability is young and partly flattered by non-recurring data-licensing deals and tax effects; FCF margins, while impressive, are still climbing off a low base.

A high discount rate is the single most important factor depressing the intrinsic value; as the company matures and volatility normalizes, a structurally lower beta/WACC would materially raise fair value (see sensitivity).

## 9. Investment Risks

**Bull Case (+30%+ upside, toward $230+):**
- Data-licensing monetization scales into a durable, high-margin annuity as AI demand for proprietary human conversation grows.
- ARPU expansion: Reddit closes the large gap to mature ad peers via better targeting, ad-load, and international monetization.
- Beta/risk-premium normalization as the company seasons post-IPO — a lower WACC alone lifts DCF fair value toward $170–$215.

**Bear Case (−30%+ downside, toward $90–$100):**
- Google algorithm/referral changes sharply curtail user-traffic growth, the single biggest top-of-funnel dependency.
- Data-licensing deals prove lumpy and one-time rather than recurring, deflating the highest-margin growth narrative.
- Growth decelerates faster than the 28% assumption while the high 14.7% discount rate persists — base-case sensitivity shows $100–$115 in that scenario.

## 10. Verdict

**Recommendation: HOLD** | **Fair-value range: $135–$170** | **Time horizon: 12 months**

Reddit is a high-quality, structurally advantaged growth franchise with a rare and genuinely scarce asset — its data — and an asset-light model that converts growth into cash with minimal capital intensity. There is much to admire. The problem is price, not business. My base-case DCF of **$137.49** sits ~22% below the current $176, indicating that the market is already discounting both sustained ~35%+ FCF growth and an eventual decline in the equity risk premium. Both may well materialize, but paying today for outcomes that require near-flawless execution offers an unfavorable margin of safety.

The crux of the call is the discount rate. At the model's 14.7% WACC (a direct function of the 1.85 beta), the stock looks expensive; but if Reddit's volatility subsides and the market applies a ~11–12% cost of capital, the same growth assumptions produce $168–$215, validating both the current price and the Street's $225 target. I am unwilling to underwrite that re-rating as a base case today, but I also see no compelling short thesis given the strength of the underlying growth and the reasonable 1.2 PEG.

Net: I would **hold existing positions** and **wait for a better entry** (a pullback toward the $135–$150 DCF zone, or evidence that data-licensing revenue is recurring) before adding. Catalysts to watch: quarterly ARPU and DAU trends, the recurrence and size of data-licensing contracts, and any moderation in stock beta that would justify a lower discount rate.
