## 2. Company Overview

GoDaddy Inc. is a leading global provider of cloud-based products and services for entrepreneurs and small-to-medium businesses, anchored by the world's largest domain-name registrar. The company operates two segments: **Applications & Commerce (A&C)** — Websites + Marketing, Managed WordPress, commerce/payments tools and marketing software — and **Core Platform (Core)** — domain registration, hosting, and security. With over 20 million customers, GoDaddy monetizes through high-retention subscriptions and is increasingly cross-selling higher-margin commerce and "presence" products, supported by its Airo AI-powered onboarding suite.

The **competitive moat** rests on scale (the dominant registrar globally), a trusted brand among non-technical small businesses, very high switching costs once a customer's domain, website, email and payments are integrated, and recurring, prepaid revenue with strong renewal economics. The business is exceptionally asset-light: trailing capex is only ~$24M against ~$1.6B of operating cash flow, producing best-in-class free-cash-flow conversion.

**Key risks** include: (1) mature top-line growth — revenue is expanding only ~6% YoY, so free-cash-flow growth depends heavily on continued margin expansion and buybacks rather than volume; (2) a heavily levered, buyback-depleted balance sheet (book equity is near zero, producing distorted ROE/P/B); (3) reliance on stock-based compensation, which inflates reported FCF relative to true owner earnings; and (4) competition from Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, Amazon and registrar price competition. The stock's 53% decline from its $183 high signals the market is pricing in decelerating growth and/or a higher risk premium.

## 9. Investment Risks

**Bull Case (+33% to upside, $114–$190+):**
- Continued operating-leverage and margin expansion drives FCF toward $2B, and the multiple re-rates toward even a modest 12–14x forward earnings.
- Airo/AI-driven attach rates lift A&C revenue mix and ARPU, reaccelerating top-line above 6%.
- Aggressive buybacks at a ~12% FCF yield are highly accretive to per-share value; deleveraging further de-risks equity.

**Bear Case (−15% to downside, ~$73 / 52-week low):**
- Revenue growth stalls or decelerates below 6% amid registrar price competition (Wix, Squarespace, Shopify, Amazon), and margin expansion exhausts — capping the FCF growth the bull case relies on.
- Stock-based-compensation-adjusted "true" FCF is materially below reported FCF, meaning the cheap optics overstate intrinsic cash returns.
- High financial leverage (near-zero book equity) amplifies equity risk if cash generation disappoints or rates stay elevated; multiple stays depressed in a "value trap."

## 10. Verdict

**Recommendation: BUY** — with a fair-value range of **$150–$190** and confidence rated **Medium**.

GoDaddy presents a compelling value setup. The shares have fallen 53% from their 52-week high to $85.83, leaving them at ~8x forward earnings, ~10.6x EV/EBITDA, and a ~12% trailing FCF yield — extraordinary for an asset-light, high-retention, cash-generative franchise that has grown free cash flow at a ~20% CAGR. Every valuation lens — discounted cash flow, peer multiples, FCF yield, and a Street that rates it Buy with a $114.53 mean target — points in the same direction: undervaluation.

We deliberately **do not** anchor on the literal $289 DCF output. That figure is inflated by a low 8.22% WACC (driven by an unusually low 0.915 beta) and by reported FCF that benefits from stock-comp add-backs. Adjusting toward a sector-equivalent discount rate (~9.5–10%) and applying conservative 6–9% perpetual FCF growth still yields $155–$250 per share — and even the harshest cell of our sensitivity grid ($155) implies ~80% upside. Triangulating the haircut DCF with the analyst high ($190) and a re-rating to a still-modest ~12–14x forward earnings, we set an actionable **$150–$190 target range**, implying roughly **75%–120% upside** over a 12–24 month horizon.

**Catalysts to watch:** (1) reacceleration of A&C/commerce revenue and ARPU via the Airo AI suite; (2) continued double-digit FCF-per-share growth aided by buybacks; (3) multiple re-rating as the market gains confidence that growth has not structurally broken; and (4) deleveraging progress. **Key things to monitor on the bear side:** any further deceleration below 6% revenue growth, evidence that margin expansion has peaked, and the magnitude of stock-based compensation relative to reported FCF. On balance, the risk/reward is favorably skewed — the downside appears anchored near the 52-week low (~$73, −15%) while multiple independent methods support substantial upside.
