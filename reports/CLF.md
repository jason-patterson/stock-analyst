## 2. Company Overview

Cleveland-Cliffs is the largest flat-rolled steel producer in North America and the largest supplier of steel to the U.S. automotive industry. Following its acquisitions of AK Steel and ArcelorMittal USA, the company became a vertically integrated producer controlling iron ore mining, pelletizing, HBI, coke, and downstream steelmaking — an unusual "mine-to-mill" model. Product lines span hot-rolled, cold-rolled and coated sheet, stainless and electrical steel (including grain-oriented GOES for transformers), plate, slab, and tubular/stamped components.

The company's competitive moat rests on scale, vertical integration (insulating it from iron-ore price spikes), and a dominant position in the auto-exposed flat-rolled market. It is also a primary beneficiary of U.S. trade protection (Section 232 tariffs) and any reshoring / infrastructure-driven demand. Management has been an aggressive consolidator of domestic steel capacity.

The key risks are structural and severe: (1) extreme operating leverage to steel spreads and auto build rates, producing wild earnings swings; (2) a **$7.78B gross debt load** against only $45M cash, making the equity highly sensitive to cyclical troughs; (3) energy-intensive blast-furnace operations facing rising decarbonization capex; and (4) customer concentration in cyclical autos. The current trough — two consecutive years of negative FCF and net losses exceeding $2.2B cumulatively (2024–2025) — illustrates the downside of this profile.

## 9. Investment Risks

**Bull Case (upside — equity is a leveraged call option):**
- A robust steel upcycle (driven by tariffs, reshoring, infrastructure, and auto recovery) could push EBITDA back toward the $3B+ 2022 peak, rapidly de-levering and re-rating the equity multiple-fold.
- As the largest flat-rolled and auto-exposed U.S. producer, CLF enjoys outsized operating leverage — small spread improvements flow disproportionately to the bottom line.
- Strategic optionality: further consolidation, asset monetization, or policy tailwinds (Section 232) could unlock value not in base fundamentals.

**Bear Case (downside):**
- The $7.78B debt load against $45M cash is the binding constraint; a prolonged trough risks covenant stress, dilutive equity raises, or distressed refinancing.
- Normalized DCF and mid-cycle EV/EBITDA both place equity value far below the current price; mean-reversion toward (or below) consensus $10.50 is the base case.
- High beta (2.0) and commodity dependence mean a recession or weak auto build cycle could drive the stock back toward its 52-week low of $6.71.

## 10. Verdict

**Recommendation: SELL / CAUTIOUS HOLD. Fair-value range ~$7–$10.**

Cleveland-Cliffs presents a textbook case of a deeply cyclical, over-leveraged industrial whose equity price has run ahead of its fundamental, cash-flow-based value. My normalized DCF — built on a full-cycle FCF of $372M and a 10.33% WACC — produces a **negative intrinsic equity value (−$3.85/share)** because enterprise value does not cover the $7.78B debt. A relative-valuation cross-check on mid-cycle EBITDA independently points to equity worth only ~$1–4/share. Both methods, plus a Street consensus target ($10.50) that already sits 23% below the market price, converge on the same conclusion: **at $13.60 the stock is pricing in a peak-cycle outcome, not a mid-cycle reality.**

The investment case for owning CLF here is essentially a leveraged, high-beta bet that a steel super-cycle (tariffs + reshoring + infrastructure + auto recovery) materializes and rapidly de-levers the balance sheet. That outcome is plausible and would be highly rewarding — the sensitivity table shows how quickly equity value inflects with growth — but it is a speculative, binary proposition rather than a margin-of-safety value investment. The asymmetry currently favors the downside given the leverage and the gap to consensus.

**Catalysts to watch:** quarterly steel HRC spreads and order books, auto production rates, any debt-reduction or refinancing actions, trade-policy developments (Section 232), and management guidance on a return to positive FCF. **Time horizon:** I would revisit constructively only on (a) a sustained move toward positive free cash flow and visible de-leveraging, or (b) a pullback toward the $7–9 range where fundamental value and cyclical optionality better align. Until then, the risk/reward at $13.60 skews unfavorable.
