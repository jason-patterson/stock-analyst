## 2. Company Overview

RTX Corporation (formerly Raytheon Technologies) is one of the world's largest aerospace and defense companies, operating three segments. **Collins Aerospace** supplies avionics, interiors, power and control systems, and aftermarket services to civil and military aircraft. **Pratt & Whitney** designs and manufactures aircraft engines (notably the Geared Turbofan / GTF) plus a large, high-margin spares and overhaul aftermarket. **Raytheon** (the defense segment) produces missiles, missile-defense systems, radars, and integrated defense electronics for the U.S. DoD and allied governments. The portfolio is roughly balanced between commercial aerospace (Collins + Pratt) and defense (Raytheon).

RTX's competitive moat is formidable: multi-decade installed bases of engines and aircraft systems generate recurring aftermarket revenue with high switching costs; long-cycle defense programs are protected by deep technological IP, security clearances, and entrenched government relationships. The company exited FY2025 with a backlog near $220B, providing multi-year revenue visibility. Pricing power, scale, and program stickiness underpin durable margins.

Key risks center on the **Pratt & Whitney GTF powder-metal recall**, which has driven multi-billion-dollar charges and pressured free cash flow over recent years; supply-chain and labor inflation in commercial aerospace; defense budget timing and continuing-resolution risk; and a leveraged balance sheet (~$39B gross debt). The stock's reported 0.30 beta understates the genuine cyclicality embedded in its commercial-aerospace exposure.

## 9. Investment Risks

**Bull Case (toward $215–242, ~+20–35%):**
- GTF recall fully resolves, unlocking ~$10B+ normalized annual FCF and re-rating the aftermarket franchise.
- Record ~$220B backlog and rising global defense spending drive sustained double-digit FCF growth.
- The market continues to assign RTX a defensive, low-beta (~0.30) profile, justifying a sub-7% discount rate.

**Bear Case (toward $87–113, ~−37% to −52%):**
- Commercial-aero cyclicality reasserts itself; a true ~0.85+ beta / 8.5%+ WACC compresses fair value to ~$113 or below.
- Further GTF or supply-chain charges delay the FCF recovery; multiple compresses from 33x toward sector norms.
- Defense budget timing/CR risk and ~$39B debt load limit capital returns and de-rate the premium.

## 10. Verdict

**Recommendation: HOLD, with a downside bias (trim into strength). Fair value range: $100–145 (base case ~$113).**

RTX is an outstanding operating franchise — diversified, moated, and backed by a record backlog — but it is not a cheap stock. Our base-case DCF of **$113.42**, built on an 8.51% WACC and 10% near-term FCF growth, implies roughly **37% downside** from the current $179.66. The crux of the call is the discount rate: the market and sell-side are implicitly treating RTX as a low-beta defensive (the reported 0.30 beta → ~6% WACC → ~$190 value), whereas we judge that RTX's substantial, cyclical commercial-aerospace exposure warrants a higher cost of capital and therefore a lower intrinsic value. At 33x trailing earnings and 18x EV/EBITDA, the shares leave virtually no margin of safety for any stumble in the GTF/FCF recovery.

We would not short a high-quality compounder with this kind of backlog and momentum, but we also cannot justify new capital at current levels. Existing holders should consider trimming; prospective buyers should wait for a pullback toward the $130–145 zone, which would offer a more attractive risk/reward.

**Catalysts to watch (12–24 months):** quarterly free-cash-flow trajectory and confirmation that GTF charges are behind the company; FY2026–2027 FCF guidance approaching ~$10B; defense book-to-bill and budget appropriations; and any beta/volatility normalization that validates (or refutes) the market's defensive framing.
